# D&D Character Sheet: Beginner's User Guide

A digital character sheet for Dungeons & Dragons (2024 rules). It runs in your browser, saves everything on your own device, and can be installed like an app.

This guide is for people who are **new to D&D** and **new to this app**. You don't need to know the rules first. The app does the math for you, and this guide explains the ideas as they come up.

## 1. D&D in five minutes

Dungeons & Dragons is a storytelling game. One player, the **Dungeon Master (DM)**, describes the world and plays everyone in it. The other players each control one hero, their **character**. You say what your character does, and when the outcome is uncertain you roll dice to find out.

Your **character sheet** is the page that records who your character is and what they can do. This app is that page, with the arithmetic done for you.

### The core idea: the d20 roll

Most of the time, the DM asks you to roll a 20-sided die (a **d20**) and add a number from your sheet. If your total meets or beats a target number (the **Difficulty Class**, or **DC**), you succeed.

- **Ability check:** trying something risky, like climbing a wall or spotting a trap.
- **Saving throw:** resisting something that happens to you, like a poison or a fireball.
- **Attack roll:** trying to hit a creature. The target number is the creature's **Armor Class (AC)**.

Sometimes you roll with **Advantage** (roll two d20s, keep the higher) or **Disadvantage** (roll two, keep the lower).

### The numbers on your sheet

| Term | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| **Ability scores** | Six numbers that describe your character: Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma. |
| **Modifier** | The bonus or penalty an ability score gives you. A score of 10 or 11 gives +0, and each 2 points above or below changes it by 1. This is what you actually add to rolls. |
| **Proficiency bonus** | A bonus you add when you are trained in something (a skill, a weapon, a saving throw). It starts at +2 and grows as you level up. |
| **Skills** | Specific things you can be good at, like Stealth or Persuasion. Each is tied to one ability. |
| **Hit Points (HP)** | How much damage you can take. At 0 HP you fall unconscious and start making death saving throws. |
| **Armor Class (AC)** | How hard you are to hit. |
| **Level** | Your overall experience. You start at level 1. Higher levels give new abilities. |
| **Class** | Your character's profession, such as Fighter, Wizard or Rogue. It decides most of what you can do. |
| **Species** | Your character's people, such as Human, Elf or Dwarf. |
| **Background** | Your character's life before adventuring. It gives skills, a tool proficiency, an Origin feat, and some starting gear. |

You don't have to memorize any of this. Hover or tap the underlined names and numbers in the app to see short explanations.

## 2. Opening the app and making a character

### First launch

When you open the app with no characters saved, you'll see three ways to begin:

- **Guided Wizard:** a step-by-step flow that explains each choice. **Start here if you're new.**
- **Roll a Random Character:** one click builds a complete character. It's good for trying the app out, for an NPC (a character the DM plays), or for inspiration. Everything can be edited afterward.
- **Skip the Wizard:** give the character a name and get a blank sheet to fill in yourself. Best if you already know the rules.

You can also import a character you exported earlier (see [Settings, backups and sharing](#11-settings-backups-and-sharing)).

### Having more than one character

Open the character switcher near your character's name (the sidebar on a computer, the page header on a phone). From there you can switch characters or open the management sheet to create a **New Character**, rename, duplicate, or delete one. Deleting asks you to confirm first.

### Installing it like an app

In Settings, the **Install App** section offers **Install on this device** when your browser supports it. You can also use your browser's own "Install" or "Add to Home Screen" option. Once installed, the app opens quickly and works without an internet connection.

## 3. The character creation wizard, step by step

The wizard walks you through the choices in the same order the 2024 rulebook does. A progress bar at the top shows where you are. Use **Next** and **Back** to move. Nothing is locked in until the end, so you can step back and change your mind.

The wizard has these steps. The **Spells** step only appears for classes that cast spells at level 1.

### Step 1: Class

Pick your **class** and give your character a **name**. A class is the biggest decision you'll make, because it decides how you fight, what you're good at, and how you play.

After you choose, the wizard shows a short guide to that class, including the ability scores it cares about most, its Hit Die, and the saving throws it is trained in. If you aren't sure which class suits you, try a few and read the guide for each. A good rule of thumb:

- **Want to hit things with a sword?** Look at Fighter, Barbarian or Paladin.
- **Want to be sneaky and clever?** Look at Rogue or Ranger.
- **Want to cast spells?** Look at Wizard, Sorcerer, Cleric, Druid, Bard or Warlock.

### Step 2: Origin

Your **Origin** is where your character comes from. You choose a **Background** and a **Species**.

**Background.** Pick one from the dropdown. A panel appears below it that explains what the background gives you:

- A short description of the background.
- Which three ability scores it can raise.
- Two skill proficiencies and one tool proficiency.
- An **Origin feat**, a small special ability. Hover or tap its name for details.
- Starting equipment: **Option A** is a package of gear plus some gold, **Option B** is 50 gold to shop with.

Open **Compare all backgrounds** to see every background side by side. Press **Choose** on any of them to pick it.

**Ability score increase.** Your background raises your ability scores. You can add **+2 to one score and +1 to another**, or **+1 to all three**. The wizard won't let you continue until the split is valid.

If the wizard shows a note that some backgrounds suit your class, that is only a suggestion. Any background works with any class.

**Species.** Pick your character's people. Some species ask for an extra choice (for example a skill, or a lineage). The wizard shows these when they apply. You may also be asked to choose languages.

### Step 3: Skills

The top of this step lists what you receive **automatically**: your class's saving throws, armor and weapon training, and the skills and feat from your background. You don't pick those.

Below that, choose the number of **class skills** your class allows. Hover or tap a skill's name to see what it's used for. If a skill is already granted by your background or species, the wizard tells you that picking it again adds nothing, because the proficiency bonus is never added twice.

Not sure? You can move on and finish later. The Overview page will remind you.

### Step 4: Ability Scores

Your six ability scores come from one of three methods:

- **Standard Array:** you get a fixed set of numbers (15, 14, 13, 12, 10, 8) and assign each to an ability. **This is the simplest option for new players.**
- **Point Buy:** you have a budget of points to spend, with higher scores costing more.
- **Rolling:** you roll dice for each score.

Put your highest numbers in the abilities your class cares about. The class guide from Step 1 tells you which. Your background's bonuses from Step 2 are added on top.

### Step 5: Spells (spellcasters only)

If your class casts spells at level 1, you pick your starting **cantrips** (spells you can cast as often as you like) and your starting leveled spells. The list only shows spells your class can actually learn. If you picked a spell that isn't legal for your class, the app tells you and lets you remove it.

### Step 6: Alignment and Details

Choose an **alignment** (a rough description of your character's moral outlook) and fill in personal details such as age, height, appearance and personality. None of this affects the math. It is there to help you play the character, and you can leave it blank for now.

**Faith / Deity (optional).** Type a god's name or pick from the list. The list covers the Greyhawk gods from the 2024 Dungeon Master's Guide plus the Forgotten Realms, the racial and dragon gods (like Moradin and Bahamut), and Dragonlance. Below the field, **Suggested deities** recommends gods that fit your class, species and alignment, and says why (for example, Lawful Good matches your alignment). Gods usually belong to one setting, so use the **Setting** menu to match your DM's world. Evil gods only appear for evil characters. These are only suggestions. Press **Choose** to fill in the Faith field, or name any god, including your own. Hover over a god's name for its symbol, alignment and a short description.

### Step 7: Equipment

In the 2024 rules, both your **class** and your **background** give you starting equipment, and any coins you get can be spent on gear right away. This step has four parts:

1. **Class equipment.** Pick a package (for example "Package A"), or take the gold instead. The Fighter has two packages plus a gold option. If a package says "choose one" (a Monk picks a tool or instrument, for example), choose it from the list.
2. **Background equipment.** Take the **Equipment Package** or **50 GP**.
3. **Buy your own gear.** Your gold from both choices above is your budget. Browse **Weapons**, **Armor** and **Gear**, search by name, and use **+** and **−** to buy or return items. Prices are the book prices. **Gold left** updates as you go, and you can hover over it to see how it was worked out.

4. **Trinket.** You can roll once on the Trinkets table (a d100) for a free, Tiny keepsake, such as a mummified goblin hand or a crystal that glows in moonlight. It has no use in the rules. It's just a bit of mystery to inspire your character's story. You can also pick one from the list if your DM allows.

A gold-only choice gives you the biggest budget. A package gives you a ready-made kit and a little spare gold to spend.

**Warnings about gear you may not use well.** If you buy something your class probably can't use properly, the app tells you. For example, a Wizard buying armor gets a warning that they aren't trained with it, and that wearing armor without training gives Disadvantage on Strength and Dexterity tests and stops spellcasting. A Rogue buying a Greatsword is told they won't add their Proficiency Bonus to its attacks. You'll also see a note for Heavy weapons or armor your Strength can't handle. These are only warnings. You can still buy the item, because a feat, a species trait or your DM's ruling might make it work.

Armor and a shield you are trained in are worn automatically when you finish. You can change what you're wearing on the Inventory page.
### Step 8: Review

A summary of everything you picked. Anything still unfinished is listed here. When you're happy, confirm and the wizard creates your character and takes you to the sheet.

## 4. Finding your way around

### Pages

The app has up to seven pages. On a computer they sit in a sidebar on the left. On a phone they sit in a bar you can swipe sideways.

| Key | Page | What it's for |
|-----|------|---------------|
| **1** | Character | Who you are: abilities, skills, saving throws, class features, a quick overview |
| **2** | Combat | Attacking, taking damage, resting, conditions, initiative |
| **4** | Spells | Spell slots, prepared spells, and a spell browser (**spellcasters only**) |
| **5** | Inventory | Weapons, armor, gear, coins, and how much you're carrying |
| **6** | Advancement | Leveling up |
| **7** | Companions | Pets, mounts, hirelings and other followers |
| **3** | Settings | Theme, dice, house rules, backups, sharing, printing |

The number keys don't follow the order on screen. That's intentional, so shortcuts stay the same as pages are added. The **Spells** page is hidden until your character can cast spells.

### Search (command palette)

Press **Ctrl + K** (or **⌘ + K** on a Mac) anywhere, or use the **Search** button. Type to jump to a page, run an action, or look up a rules term such as a condition. Press **Enter** to go.

### Keyboard shortcuts

The **Shortcuts** button shows the full list. The short version:

- **1 to 7:** go to a page.
- **Ctrl / ⌘ + K:** open search.

### Character Assistant

The **Assistant** button next to Search opens the **Character Assistant**. It looks at your sheet and offers suggestions, such as unfinished choices. It only suggests. It never changes your character without you pressing a button. If it has nothing to say, it tells you "No suggestions right now."

### Help and tooltips

- **Underlined or dotted numbers** (AC, HP, skill bonuses, spell save DC and so on) show **how they were calculated** when you hover over them or tap them, for example `10 + 2 + 2 = 14`. This is the quickest way to learn why a number is what it is.
- **Named things** (skills, saving throws, spells, feats, class features, languages, backgrounds) show a short explanation in the same way.
- Some pages have a small **?** help bubble that explains the page.

On a touch screen, tap to open a tooltip and tap elsewhere to close it.

## 5. The Character page

The Character page has three sub-tabs: **Overview**, **Abilities & Skills**, and **Class & Features**.

### Overview

Your at-a-glance page, and the one you'll use most during play. From top to bottom you'll find:

- **Personal details:** name, species, size, class, level, background, alignment, faith, and appearance. This is where you edit them.
- **Finish setting up:** a checklist of choices you haven't made yet, such as skills or spells. It disappears when everything is done.
- **Vitals:** hit points, armor class, speed, initiative, passive Perception, proficiency bonus, Hit Dice and more.
- **Heroic Inspiration:** a toggle. In D&D 2024 your DM may award you Heroic Inspiration, which lets you reroll a die once.
- **Conditions:** anything currently affecting you, such as Poisoned or Prone.
- **Reactions:** things you can do in response to an event on someone else's turn.
- **Resources:** limited-use abilities such as "uses per rest".
- **Attacks:** your weapons and their attack and damage numbers.
- **Spellcasting:** your spell save DC and spell attack bonus (spellcasters only).
- **Skills:** a quick-reference list.
- **Traits and feats:** what your species, class and feats give you, plus the languages you know.

### Abilities & Skills

- **Ability Scores:** each score with its modifier. Hover over a modifier to see how it's worked out.
- **Saving Throws:** your bonus for each of the six, with a marker where you're proficient.
- **Skills:** all skills with your total bonus. Proficient skills get your proficiency bonus added. Some characters get **Expertise**, which doubles that bonus for chosen skills.

### Class & Features

Your class and everything it gives you, listed by the level at which you gained it. Use this to look up what an ability does.

Where a feature offers a choice you haven't made yet (such as a fighting style), look for it under **Choices Pending** on the **Advancement** page.

## 6. The Combat page

The Combat page is a quick reference for the table. Your DM runs the fight and you roll your own dice, so the page doesn't take turns for you. It keeps the numbers you look up during a fight in one place and handles the small things worth counting: hit points, saving throws, conditions, effects, ammo and rounds.

### The summary strip

A strip across the top stays visible as you scroll. It shows your hit points (with any temporary hit points), Armor Class and Speed, the round and your initiative once a fight is on, and the spell you are concentrating on. Tap or hover over a number to see how it was worked out.

### Fight

- **Start fight** begins a round counter at round 1.
- **Next round** adds a round and counts down every timed condition and effect for you. When something runs out it is removed, and a line under the buttons says what ended (for example "Round 4. Ended: Poisoned, Bless.").
- **Roll initiative** rolls a Dexterity check with your bonus (and Advantage or Disadvantage where your conditions give it), or lets you type in the number from a physical die. It keeps the result in the strip until the fight ends. Rolling initiative starts a fight if one isn't on.
- **End fight** clears the round and your initiative. Timers on conditions and effects keep their values.

### Status

- **Hit Points and Take Damage:** type damage and its type, mark resistance or vulnerability for that hit, and heal or add temporary hit points. Temporary hit points are used up first and don't stack. If damage breaks your concentration, the app prompts you for the Constitution save.
- **Death Saves:** appears when you are at 0 HP. Roll each save and the pips fill in.
- **Saving Throws:** all six saves with your bonus and a **Roll** button each. A save shows **Auto-fail** when a condition like Paralyzed or Stunned makes you fail Strength and Dexterity saves, and **Disadvantage** when a condition like Restrained gives it (a Dexterity save). The roll starts with that Disadvantage already selected, and Exhaustion is applied. The result is shown under the saves. The app doesn't compare it to a DC; your DM tells you whether it worked.
- **Active Conditions:** add a condition, read what it does, and give it a duration in rounds, minutes or hours, or until a rest or while you concentrate. Timed durations count down with **Next round**, and you can take a round off by hand.
- **Effects:** track spells and features that last a number of rounds, such as Bless or Rage (1 minute is 10 rounds). Give it a name and an optional number of rounds. **Next round** counts them down, **-1** and **+1** adjust by hand, and **End** removes one. The app doesn't apply what an effect does; it only keeps the count.
- **Concentration:** shows what you are concentrating on and ends it when you start another.
- **Defenses & Combat Stats:** Armor Class, Speed, Hit Point Maximum, Initiative bonus and Passive Perception, with an **Adjust** area for an Armor Class override and an initiative bonus.

### Attacks

Each equipped weapon (and your Unarmed Strike) shows its attack bonus, damage, properties and mastery, with two buttons:

- **Attack** rolls a d20 with your attack bonus (or lets you type a physical die) and shows the total to tell your DM. The app doesn't check it against Armor Class; your DM says whether it hits. A natural 20 is a Critical Hit and a natural 1 misses. Conditions you have set the starting roll: Poisoned starts it with Disadvantage, for example. Exhaustion is applied.
- **Damage** rolls the weapon's damage. Tick **Critical hit** to double the dice (it's ticked for you after a natural 20). With manual dice, it tells you what to roll instead.

A weapon that uses ammunition, like a bow, shows how much you have left right on its card. Each attack roll uses one piece. When you are out, the Attack button turns off. The **Ammunition** card counts what you fired this fight and has **Recover half** for after the fight (half of what you fired, rounded down).

### Spells and turn options

- **Spellcasting** (spellcasters only): your spell save DC and spell attack bonus, your spell slots as circles you tap to spend or get back, and the cantrips and prepared spells ready to cast, with their casting time and Concentration or Ritual tags. Tap a spell's name for the full description.
- **On your turn:** the standard actions anyone can take (Attack, Dash, Disengage, Dodge, Help, Hide, Influence, Magic, Ready, Search, Study, Utilize), your Bonus Action spells, and the Reactions you have, such as Opportunity Attack or Shield, with a note when one isn't available right now. Tap a name for what it does. Class features that use a Bonus Action are on the Class & Features tab.
### Resources and rests

Class resources (such as Rage or Channel Divinity) with their counters, your Hit Dice, and the **Short Rest** and **Long Rest** buttons. A Long Rest dialog lists exactly what resets.
## 7. The Spells page

This page appears only when your character can cast spells. (Some subclasses, like Eldritch Knight and Arcane Trickster, gain it at level 3.)

Spells are grouped into **cantrips** (cast freely) and **leveled spells** (1st level and up), which use up a **spell slot** each time you cast them. Slots come back after a Long Rest.

The sub-tabs are:

- **Summary:** everything about your spellcasting in one place. It shows your spellcasting ability, spell save DC and spell attack bonus (the save DC is the number enemies must beat to resist your spells, and the app shows how it is worked out), your **spell slots** by level (mark them as you spend them), and **Your spells**: every cantrip you know and every spell you have prepared, grouped by level. Tap a name for its details. Use the button at the bottom to jump to the Prepared tab to change them.
- **Prepared:** your cantrips and the spells you've prepared. Not every class prepares spells the same way: some prepare a number of spells from their whole list each day, while others know a fixed set. Wizards also keep a **Spellbook**: it starts with six spells and gains two more with each Wizard level after 1. The page shows how many of those free spells you have, and gently notes it if your book holds more (extra spells have to be copied in from scrolls or other books, so check with your DM). It never stops you from keeping them.
- **Browse:** search the spell list. You'll only see spells your class can learn.

Spell names throughout the app are underlined. Tap one to see its casting time, range, components and effect.

Characters with more than one spellcasting class get a **Spellcasting by Class** breakdown. Warlocks use **Pact Magic**, which works a little differently, and the app shows it separately.

## 8. The Inventory page

Your gear, split into:

- **Weapons:** add weapons from the catalog. Each shows its damage, properties and mastery.
- **Armor & Shields:** add what you own and choose what you're **wearing**. Your Armor Class updates automatically. The tooltip on AC shows the full breakdown.
- **Other Gear:** everything else, with quantities and weights.
- **Coin Purse:** platinum, gold, electrum, silver and copper.
- **Encumbrance:** how much you carry compared with your capacity, plus **Weight by Category**.

Use the **Add Weapon**, **Add Armor** and **Add Gear** buttons to pick from the catalog.

The standard 2024 rules track carrying capacity, but don't use the older "variant encumbrance" penalties. If your table wants those, turn on the house rule in [Settings](#11-settings-backups-and-sharing).

## 9. The Advancement page: leveling up

When your character has gained enough experience, they **level up**. This page handles it.

### Choose how you level

At the top, pick a **leveling mode**:

- **Milestone** (the default): your DM tells you when you level up. Most groups play this way.
- **XP:** the page tracks **experience points**. Enter an amount (and an optional reason) to **Award** XP. When you have enough, you can level up.

### Level & Experience (and levelling up)

The top card shows your level and, in XP mode, your progress toward the next level. Below that, in the same card, the **Next Level** part shows what your next level gives you and lets you advance. Level-ups can include:

- New class features.
- A **subclass** (a specialization, usually chosen at level 3).
- **Ability Score Improvements or feats** at certain levels.
- More spell slots or spells.

### Progression

The **Progression** card tells you where you stand and what is next. In XP mode it shows a progress bar toward your next level. **What to do now** lists anything waiting for you, such as a subclass or feat to choose or a level you can take, and each item has a link to the place where you do it. **Coming up** looks ahead to your next feat slot and your subclass choice.

### Hit Points

When you level up, you also gain hit points. Choose to **Take fixed value** (the safe average) or **Roll the Hit Die** (random, could be better or worse). Your Constitution modifier is added either way.

### Multiclass

You can take levels in a **second class** instead of your current one. The 2024 rules require minimum ability scores for this, and the app checks them. A house rule in Settings lets your table bend that.

### Choices Pending and Class Choices

Some features ask you to make a choice, such as **Expertise** skills, a **Fighting Style**, a **language**, a **subclass**, a **feat**, spells, or class options like **Metamagic** or **Eldritch Invocations**. When you owe any choices, the **Choices Pending** card moves to the **top** of the page with a gold border and the heading **Action needed**, so you can't miss it. Work through the items in it. Each one either lets you pick right there or links to the page where you make the choice. The list gets shorter as you finish, and when nothing is owed the card drops to a quiet note near the bottom. The **Advancement** tab in the main menu also shows a small gold badge with the number of choices you owe, on every page, so you know there is something to do before you open it. The Overview's **Finish setting up** card also reminds you.

### Advancement Log

A history of your level-ups and XP awards, if you want to look back.

## 10. The Companions page

This page is for permanent followers. It has five sub-tabs:

- **Companions:** a familiar, a steed, a Primal Companion or another permanent creature, chosen from a catalog. Each has its own hit points and conditions.
- **Mounts & Animals:** riding and pack animals, with carrying capacity.
- **Hirelings:** people you pay for help. You can fill in a stat block of your own.
- **NPC Party Members:** non-player characters who travel with the party.
- **Sidekicks:** simple companion characters. This sub-tab is an optional rule and must be turned on in Settings.

Temporary summoned creatures from spells are not tracked here. Companion hit points and conditions are edited on this page only.

## 11. Settings, backups and sharing

### Appearance

Switch between **dark** and **light** themes. Your choice is remembered on this device.

### Dice Rolling

By default the app rolls virtual dice for you. Turn on **Enter dice results manually** to roll real dice at the table and type the result in instead.

### House Rules

House rules are **optional** changes that your table agrees to. They are **off by default**, so the app follows the official rules until you switch one on:

- **Variant Encumbrance:** a stricter carrying system.
- **Allow Illegal Multiclass:** lets you multiclass even if you don't meet the ability score requirement.
- **Bonus Feats at Level 20:** extra feats beyond level 20.
- **Sidekicks:** enables the Sidekicks sub-tab.
- **Loyalty:** an optional followers' loyalty meter.

Active house rules are shown as an indicator on the Combat page so you don't forget they're on.

### Data & Backup

Your characters are stored **only in this browser, on this device**. Nothing is sent to a server. That is private, but it also means that clearing your browser data, or switching devices, can lose them. So back up.

- **Export current character:** downloads your character as a file.
- **Restore from backup file:** loads a previously exported file. The app asks you to confirm before it replaces anything.
- **Automatic backups:** turn this on if you want the app to keep backups for you.

### Share (read-only link)

**Copy read-only share link** gives you a link that opens a read-only view of the character. It's handy for sending your sheet to your DM. The recipient can look but can't change anything. Very long links might not open everywhere. If so, share an exported file instead.

### Print

**Print character sheet** produces a paper copy, for when you want a physical sheet at the table.

## 12. Tips and tricks

- **Not sure where a number comes from?** Hover over or tap it.
- **Not sure what a word means?** Press Ctrl / ⌘ + K and search for it, or check the [Glossary](#13-glossary).
- **Stuck on a choice?** The wizard's class guide and the **Compare all backgrounds** list are there for you. You can also ask your DM, since your DM knows the game you're playing.
- **Playing at the table?** The **Overview** and **Combat** pages cover most of what you need. Learn the 1 and 2 keys to flip between them.
- **Back up before big changes** like leveling up or importing. It takes two clicks.
- **Rolling a random character** is a fast way to see what a finished sheet looks like. Delete it afterwards.
- **Your DM has the final say.** The app follows the official 2024 rules. If your table plays differently, use the house rules or just note it with your DM.

## 13. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Action** | The main thing you do on your turn, such as attacking or casting a spell. |
| **Advantage / Disadvantage** | Roll two d20s and take the higher (advantage) or lower (disadvantage). |
| **Alignment** | A rough label for your character's moral outlook. |
| **AC (Armor Class)** | The number an attack must meet or beat to hit you. |
| **Bonus action** | A quick extra action. You only get one on your turn, and only if something gives you one. |
| **Cantrip** | A spell you can cast at will, with no spell slot. |
| **Concentration** | Holding a spell's effect. Only one concentration spell at a time. |
| **Condition** | A status effect such as Poisoned or Prone. |
| **DC (Difficulty Class)** | The target number for a check or save. |
| **DM (Dungeon Master)** | The player who runs the game. |
| **Feat** | A special ability, gained from your background or at certain levels. |
| **Hit Dice** | Dice you spend during a Short Rest to recover hit points. |
| **HP (Hit Points)** | How much damage you can take before falling. |
| **Initiative** | A roll that decides who acts first in a fight. |
| **Modifier** | The bonus or penalty from an ability score. |
| **NPC** | Non-player character, played by the DM. |
| **Proficiency bonus** | The bonus you add for things you're trained in. |
| **Reaction** | A response you can make on someone else's turn. One per round. |
| **Saving throw** | A roll to resist an effect. |
| **Spell slot** | A resource spent to cast a leveled spell. |
| **Subclass** | A specialization within your class. |
| **Temporary hit points** | A buffer of extra hit points that is lost first and doesn't stack. |
| **Weapon Mastery** | A 2024 feature that unlocks a special property of certain weapons. |

## 14. Troubleshooting

**My character disappeared.**
Characters live in your browser's storage. If you cleared site data, used a private window, or switched browser or device, they won't be there. Restore from a backup file in Settings. This is why backups matter.

**The Spells page is missing.**
It only appears when your character can cast spells. Some classes gain spellcasting at level 3.

**A number looks wrong.**
Hover over or tap it for the breakdown. The cause is usually a piece of equipment that isn't marked as worn, a missing proficiency, or an unfinished choice.

**A tooltip won't open or close on my phone.**
Tap the underlined text to open it, and tap anywhere else to close it.

**The share link doesn't open.**
It may be too long for some apps. Export the character to a file and send that instead.

**I picked the wrong thing in the wizard.**
Use **Back** to return to the step. Once the character is created, most things can be edited on the sheet itself. Your class and level are changed from the Character and Advancement pages.
